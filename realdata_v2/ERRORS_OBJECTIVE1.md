# Errors and risks in the first research objective (CKINN)

Reviewed 2026-11-21: `CKINN_OBJ1.pdf` (original proposal, ChatGPT transcript, 26 pages, sections 1 to 28), `CKINN_28-Step_Protocol.pdf` (11 pages), `COVERAGE_28_STEPS.md` (my earlier coverage review), and the real-data notebook. Page numbers refer to the PDF page footers. Errors in the version 1 real-data notebook are already documented in `AUDIT.md` (findings F1 to F20) and are summarised in part D. Corrections made to the version 2 notebook after its full run are in `protocol_v2.md`, section 10.

Severity: **critical** = would invalidate a result or claim; **major** = weakens a claim or makes it untestable; **minor** = wording, clarity or presentation.

## A. Original proposal (CKINN_OBJ1.pdf)

## B. 28-step protocol (CKINN_28-Step_Protocol.pdf)

| # | Where | Error | Severity | How the notebook handles it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | whole document | Written for the synthetic cohorts S1_JP, S2_CN, S3_US: it names columns and files that do not exist in the real data (`undiag_dm`, `incident_dm`, `lab_dm`, `sim_state`, `on_glucose_med`, `cohort_config.json`, "fifteen files"). It cannot be followed literally on real data. | Major | Mapped to NAGALA, RICH and NHANES in COVERAGE_28_STEPS.md. |
| B2 | p.11 | The PDF is truncated: page 11 ends in the middle of step 27 and there is no step 28, although the title says 28 steps. | Major | Re-export needed; step 28 marked "not in the PDF". |
| B3 | step 3, p.2 | Makes detection (task A) the primary task "because Objective 1 is detection", while the proposal recommends future risk (B) or progression (C) (s.3, p.4) and RQ1 asks about future risk. The two documents contradict each other on the primary task. | Major | Both tasks are analysed; the choice of primary task for the thesis is the investigator's and supervisor's. |
| B4 | step 3, p.2 | Uses FPG and HbA1c as inputs for the incident task. In NAGALA and RICH the outcome is defined from the same measurements, so they dominate the model. Not an error, but a design risk. | Minor | T3 sensitivity analysis. |
| B5 | step 9, p.4 | The lambda grid {0, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10} includes 0 (CKINN would equal the MLP) and gives no rule for near ties. | Major | Declared rule; grid starts above 0. |
| B6 | step 12, p.5 | The shared features (age, sex, BMI, SBP, DBP, smoker, alcohol, family history) do not exist in both real cohorts: NAGALA lacks family history, RICH alcohol is unused, smoking is coded differently; and dropping all laboratory values discards predictors both cohorts share. | Major | Ten predictors present in both cohorts, including TG, HDL, TC, ALT, AST. |
| B7 | step 13, p.6 | "One primary endpoint" is two metrics (AUROC and calibration slope), measured on S3; on real data S3 (NHANES) has a different outcome, so the endpoint cannot be computed. | Major | H1 (AUROC non-inferiority) is the frozen primary family. |
| B8 | step 2, p.2 | Setting BMI to 24 and to 35 in held-out rows creates implausible people (BMI 35 with a small waist); the violation count then depends on combinations the model never saw, and only BMI is tested. | Minor | Post hoc cell runs it as specified, beside derivative- and perturbation-based rates for all constrained predictors. |
| B9 | step 16, p.7 | Gaps in sensitivity at one fixed threshold reflect differences in base risk between groups (shown in both real-data runs: RICH age-band gaps of about 0.4 to 0.5 for every model). | Major | Sensitivity at matched specificity added. |
| B10 | step 20, p.8 | M4 adds a cohort indicator, which has no valid value for a new population, so M4 cannot be applied to the external set it is meant to help. | Major | Post hoc cell evaluates M4 within the two training cohorts only and says so. |
| B11 | step 23, p.10 | "Accept only if the interval excludes zero" across five hypotheses and several cohorts without multiplicity correction; "CKINN beats MLP" does not say which MLP. | Major | Holm correction; families A (vs M2) and B (vs M1). |
| B12 | steps 8 and 24, p.4 and p.10 | Prevalence "3.7% to 6%" is the synthetic value; real prevalences are lower (about 2.0% to 4.2%). | Minor | Real prevalences reported in table 01. |
| B13 | step 6, p.3 | Asks for one citation per row but gives none; leaves smoking unconstrained as "weak or non-monotone" without a reference. | Minor | Verified references added for the constrained rows; the smoking statement remains uncited. |
| B14 | step 10, p.5 | The stress-test subgroup (65 and over, BMI under 20) is the synthetic generator's built-in exception; in real data it is tiny (NAGALA 32 people, 1 event). | Minor | Post hoc cell reports it with the 20-event rule (not estimable in NAGALA). |
| B15 | step 14, p.6 | Attribution fidelity to generator weights exists only for synthetic data. | Minor | Replaced by stability and sign checks. |
| B16 | step 21, p.9 | "Per-cohort standardisation from training statistics only" is contradictory for a new cohort that has no training rows; standardising a test cohort with its own statistics uses test data. | Major | Training statistics only (amendment A17). |
| B17 | step 19, p.8 | "Fit the SVM on a subsample if it is too slow" gives the SVM less training data than the other models and biases the comparison (v1: AUROC 0.749 in RICH with 8,000 rows). | Minor | Post hoc SVM uses a kernel approximation on all training rows. |

## D. Version 1 real-data notebook (summary of AUDIT.md)

The only version 1 PASS (H4) was circular (F1); CKINN and M2 were not paired (F2); the transport drop was ill-posed (F3); one split and one seed (F4); lambda chosen at the grid boundary by noise (F5); unequal tuning (F6); normal-approximation p-values and a mis-described Holm family (F7); a base-rate-driven fairness statistic (F8); M1 mislabelled and confounded with augmentation (F9); a missingness endpoint that favoured augmented models (F10); RICH training capped at 40,000 rows (F11); transductive pooled standardisation (F12); NHANES label including treated people (119 of 1,432 positives) and short fasts (F13); a non-random NHANES extract (F14); unequal outcome windows (F15); under-recorded RICH family history (F16); unused drinking status (F17); raw data inside the outputs archive (F18); reporting and engineering defects (F19); weak or capped comparators (F20).
