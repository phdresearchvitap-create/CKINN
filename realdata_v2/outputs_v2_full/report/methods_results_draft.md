## Methods

**Design and amendments.** This is a re-analysis (version 2) of a study whose version 1 results had been seen. All changes were fixed in a protocol before any version 2 result existed; each is listed with its reason in the amendment log (protocol_v2.md, section 2), and analyses are labelled as pre-specified in version 1, pre-specified in version 2, or exploratory.

**Data.** Three public, de-identified datasets were used: the NAGALA cohort (Japan; 15,464 participants, 373 incident diabetes events), the Rich Healthcare Group cohort (China, 15 sites; 211,833 participants, 4,174 events) and an NHANES extract (USA, cycles 1999 to 2018), in which undiagnosed diabetes was defined as fasting plasma glucose of at least 7.0 mmol/L after at least 8 hours of fasting or HbA1c of at least 6.5% among adults reporting no diagnosis and no glucose-lowering medication (29,410 labelled adults, 1,241 positive). Predictors were routine clinical and laboratory measurements without glucose or HbA1c (tier T2); a tier adding them (T3) was a sensitivity analysis in the two cohorts. Exact zeros in laboratory values were treated as not measured. In each cohort 20% of participants (stratified) formed a development subset used only for tuning; the remainder formed the evaluation subset.

**Models.** M1 is a multilayer network with per-group encoders in which missing values take the training mean; M2 is the same network with the missingness mask as input and training-time missingness augmentation; CKINN is M2 with a penalty on input derivatives whose sign contradicts a fixed clinical sign table (risk increasing with age, body-mass index, waist, systolic pressure, family history and triglycerides; decreasing with HDL cholesterol and regular exercise). Within a fold, M1, M2 and CKINN shared the seed, the inner validation split and the training rows, so that CKINN with lambda 0 is identical to M2. Competitors were L2 logistic regression, sign-constrained logistic regression, a spline additive logistic model, random forest, XGBoost and LightGBM with and without monotone constraints from the same sign table, and multiple imputation by chained equations (5 imputations) followed by logistic regression.

**Tuning.** On the development subset, M1 and M2 each evaluated 32 configurations; CKINN took M2's best configuration among the first 24 and evaluated 8 values of lambda, the same budget of 32 evaluations. Lambda was the largest value whose development score was within one standard error of the best score among lambda 0 and the grid (declared before any version 2 result).

**Evaluation.** Each model was evaluated by 5 repeats of stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the evaluation subset, with network seed r in repeat r; in repeat 0 the networks were also trained with 4 further seeds. Metrics were computed on the pooled out-of-fold predictions of each run and averaged over runs. Uncertainty came from a paired participant bootstrap (2,000 draws) applied identically to all runs and models; one-sided p-values were read from the draws. DeLong's test on one run, the corrected resampled t-test over the 25 folds and, in the Rich cohort, a cluster bootstrap over sites were cross-checks. Two hypothesis families (CKINN minus M2, primary; CKINN minus M1) were each corrected by Holm's method across their primary endpoints: H1 AUROC non-inferiority (margin 0.01); H2 Brier score and distance of the calibration slope from 1; H3 the transport gap (AUROC of the same model class cross-validated inside the target cohort minus the AUROC of the source-trained model applied unchanged) and absolute calibration-in-the-large in the target; H4 the agreement of integrated-gradient attributions across seeds; H5 the sex gap and age-band range of sensitivity at a false-positive rate of 0.1. Secondary analyses covered calibration (slope, calibration-in-the-large, expected calibration error and reliability curves), decision curves, censoring-aware metrics (Harrell's and Uno's C and the inverse-probability-of-censoring weighted AUROC at 3 years), subgroup metrics, missingness injected at test time, leave-one-site-out validation, constraint ablations with wrong-sign controls, empirical sign checks against held-out partial dependence, and transport with recalibration, reweighting and pooled training. ## Results

**Verdicts.** In family A (CKINN minus M2), H1 was PASS, H2 NULL, H3 NULL, H4 PASS and H5 NULL. In family B (CKINN minus M1), H1 was PASS, H2 NULL, H3 NULL, H4 NULL and H5 NULL.

**CKINN versus M2.** NAGALA: AUROC difference 0.0024 (95% CI -0.0005 to 0.0054; non-inferiority predicted), Brier difference -0.00005 (-0.00011 to 0.00000); RICH: AUROC difference 0.0002 (95% CI -0.0002 to 0.0006; non-inferiority predicted), Brier difference -0.00001 (-0.00002 to 0.00000); NHANES: AUROC difference 0.0003 (95% CI -0.0005 to 0.0011; non-inferiority predicted), Brier difference -0.00001 (-0.00004 to 0.00002).

**CKINN versus M1.** NAGALA: AUROC difference 0.0028 (95% CI -0.0006 to 0.0064; non-inferiority predicted), Brier difference -0.00003 (-0.00009 to 0.00004); RICH: AUROC difference -0.0006 (95% CI -0.0014 to 0.0001; non-inferiority predicted), Brier difference 0.00000 (-0.00001 to 0.00002); NHANES: AUROC difference 0.0054 (95% CI 0.0026 to 0.0084; non-inferiority predicted), Brier difference -0.00017 (-0.00029 to -0.00006).

**Best-performing models.** NAGALA: highest run-averaged AUROC cLR (0.8057); lowest Brier cLR (0.02223). NHANES: highest run-averaged AUROC GAM (0.8271); lowest Brier CKINN (0.03741). RICH: highest run-averaged AUROC M1 (0.8494); lowest Brier M1 (0.01841).

**Power.** For NAGALA, the bootstrap standard deviation of the CKINN minus M2 AUROC difference was 0.0015, so the difference detectable with 80% power at two-sided 5% is 0.0042, and the power of the non-inferiority test (margin 0.01) if the true difference were zero is 1.000. Both ignore the Holm correction and the variability of model training, so they overstate the power of the actual decision.

**Transport.** NAGALA to RICH, M1: target cross-validated AUROC 0.848, external 0.814, gap 0.034; NAGALA to RICH, M2: target cross-validated AUROC 0.848, external 0.815, gap 0.033; NAGALA to RICH, CKINN: target cross-validated AUROC 0.848, external 0.827, gap 0.022; RICH to NAGALA, M1: target cross-validated AUROC 0.802, external 0.788, gap 0.014; RICH to NAGALA, M2: target cross-validated AUROC 0.802, external 0.789, gap 0.013; RICH to NAGALA, CKINN: target cross-validated AUROC 0.802, external 0.795, gap 0.007.

## Limitations
- Version 1 results were seen before every v2 decision; v2 is a re-analysis of the same development and evaluation rows, not a new test set. All amendments are listed in protocol_v2.md, section 2.
- Task B labels are binary incident diabetes over follow-up windows that differ between cohorts (NAGALA 0.4 to 13.0 years, RICH 2.0 to 7.6 years; AUDIT F15); transport differences mix population shift with design. The censoring-aware analyses address the horizon only in part.
- Cross-population evidence is from Japan and China; NHANES is a detection task, its extract is a non-random subset of the survey (AUDIT F14), and survey-weighted estimates do not represent the US population.
- RICH family history is probably under-recorded at several sites (AUDIT F16); RICH drinking status is unused (AUDIT F17).
- Missingness experiments remove values at test time; the training augmentation of M2, CKINN, LR_aug and XGB_aug matches the primary MCAR scenario, which favours those models against the other comparators (AUDIT F10).
- H4 is now measured by across-seed attribution stability, which CKINN is not trained for; the violation rates remain a manipulation check and are not evidence of clinical validity. Agreement with clinical priors is not proof that a model is causally correct.
- Subgroup gaps at one common threshold reflect differences in risk between groups; sensitivity at matched specificity isolates ranking within groups but subgroups with few events have wide intervals.
- A 'seed' changes the network initialisation, the inner validation split and the batch order together, so the seed variance component includes inner-split variance. M1, which has no augmentation, sees its mini-batches in a different order from M2 and CKINN after the first epoch.
- Lambda was chosen at the top of the pre-declared grid in every cohort and tier: the development score barely changed across the grid and the declared rule takes the largest lambda within one standard error, so CKINN here is a strongly constrained network.
- Bootstrap intervals hold the fitted models fixed; the corrected repeated-CV t-test, which allows for training variability, gives wider intervals. Intervals for the IPCW time-dependent AUROC hold the censoring distribution fixed.
- The post hoc analyses (label PH; section 12 of report.md) were decided after the v2 results were seen; they describe the models further and cannot support a confirmatory claim. The reasoning texts and SHAP values are model behaviour, not causal effects or advice.
- Outputs are risk estimates from retrospective research data; they are not diagnoses and have not been validated for clinical use.
