# Objective 1 · Operational reanalysis protocol

## What the proposal asks

Pages 3–4 distinguish detecting current diabetes, predicting future diabetes and modelling transitions through prediabetes. Pages 7–10 draw a branched predictor, specify a squared wrong-direction gradient penalty and explicitly favor soft constraints. Pages 12–22 ask about transport, calibration, explanations and group-specific errors. The proposal supplies the questions. This implementation fixes the three-year horizon, eligibility, features, penalty support, budgets, primary contrast and analysis rules for this reanalysis. Earlier analyses have been discussed. There is no dated prospective registration in the supplied material.

The study can estimate prediction error for recorded diabetes, examine whether a soft penalty changes local model behavior, and compare performance in two selected examination cohorts. The survey extracts support a separate, cross-sectional question about a single measured HbA1c. Clinical subtype adjudication, repeated state transitions, insulin–glucose dynamics, expert agreement, treatment effects and patient benefit require additional data. The graph on pages 24–25 and physiological model on pages 10–11 and 26 are future extensions. Publication and clinical utility are independent questions.

## A · Recorded incident diabetes

The index is the baseline examination. The event is the first recorded qualifying detection within three years. RICH supplies all fitting, tuning and threshold choices. NAGALA supplies a frozen external evaluation. These are selected adults attending repeated health examinations. The estimand conditions on the source cohort's observation and inclusion criteria. Transport to people without follow-up visits, a general population, other clinics or patients on medications requires separate evidence.

**RICH.** Chen et al. describe health checks across 32 locations in 11 cities, age ≥20, at least two visits and exclusion of intervals below two years. Fasting glucose ≥7 mmol/L or self-reported diabetes identified incident cases. Observation stopped at diagnosis or the final visit, whichever came first. These source exclusions may induce selection, including sparse ascertainment in the first two years. Visit-level records are unavailable. [Original RICH study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6169758/) · [deposited data](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.ft8750v)

**NAGALA.** The original longitudinal study and deposit establish the population and workbook. The user-supplied Zha et al. analysis of the same deposit explicitly identifies the first qualifying visit or first report as the detection date, and the final visit as the censoring date. It describes more frequent examinations for many participants and original restrictions involving medication use, alcohol, liver disease and baseline glycaemia. We use the deposited incident flag and days divided by 365.25. The original abstract and workbook header alone do not specify the stopping rule. The explicit operational corroboration comes from a secondary publication. The flag also reflects ascertainment with HbA1c, unavailable in RICH. A qualifying visit can occur after biological onset. Neither file gives individual visit histories for reconstructing interval censoring. [Original NAGALA study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41366-018-0076-3) · [author deposit](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.8q0p192) · [explicit timing methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-78672-0)

Apply age ≥20, finite positive follow-up, binary incident flag, observed positive FPG <7 mmol/L and documented baseline source exclusions. Require measured 0 < HbA1c <6.5% in NAGALA. Then restrict FPG to <6.1 mmol/L in both cohorts. This last restriction is an operational transport choice. The broader RICH sensitivity retains 6.1 to <7.0 mmol/L. RICH cannot implement a parallel HbA1c exclusion. Preserve the original duration for every evaluation.

## The fixed-horizon model and its objective

With observed time T, event E and τ=3, E=1 and T≤τ defines a case. Anyone with T>τ is a control even if diabetes is subsequently detected. Everyone else has unknown three-year status. Their stored zero response is a computational placeholder paired with exactly zero supervised weight. They can still inform observed-input clinical penalties.

Estimate censoring survival G by reverse Kaplan–Meier. At every tied time, remove incident detections before computing the conditional censoring decrement. Cases use 1/G(T−), controls use 1/G(3), and unknowns receive zero. G(3) is right-continuous. Original times are retained. Refuse to estimate a marginal horizon measure without beyond-horizon support. Training and evaluation estimate separate G within their corresponding partitions. Estimated external G enters metrics only. The identifying assumption is independent censoring under the chosen estimator and adequate positivity. No primary weights are truncated. Inspect G, positive-weight quantiles, maximum, risk-set size and ESS = (Σw)²/Σw². [Graf et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10474158/) · [reverse-KM tie convention](https://scikit-survival.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/generated/sksurv.nonparametric.kaplan_meier_estimator.html)

The optional site-specific G sensitivity pools sites with fewer than 30 observations beyond three years into the stratum with the greatest number beyond three years, using only the corresponding nuisance-estimation partition. It requires one supported stratum. This borrows censoring information and assumes comparable follow-up within the pooled stratum. It cannot identify site-specific risk at sites with no horizon support. Compare M3 and site-weighted-G fits under the same marginal metric and again under the same conditional metric. Site-held-out validation is separate.

Default branches consume 4, 4 and 8 inputs including masks. Each linear layer and SiLU yields eight values. Concatenate the 24 values and apply 24→32, SiLU, dropout, 32→16, SiLU and 16→1. The final number is a logit. Its sigmoid is a probability for this single horizon. The default parameter count is 152 in the branches and 1,345 in fusion. Parameter counts are logged for each fitted configuration. A four-input branched model uses a constant zero input for the empty laboratory branch, keeping the three-branch fusion topology. This branch contains only a baseline offset. The single-stream variant changes topology with the same clinical inputs.

For minibatch size N, compute Ldata = Σ wᵢ BCEWithLogits(logitᵢ,yᵢ)/N. Every row participates in the denominator. For constrained feature j with sign sⱼ, Lclinical averages max(0,−sⱼ ∂p/∂zⱼ)² over eligible participant-feature pairs in that batch. A pair qualifies only if observed and within inclusive training 1st–99th percentiles of that feature. Empty sets contribute zero. For x in clinical units, ∂p/∂xⱼ = (∂p/∂zⱼ)/scaleⱼ. Fixed masks and positive scales preserve signs. Training uses autodifferentiation with create_graph=True so this term can change parameter updates. Use a shared forward pass with dropout active for both training terms. Evaluation disables dropout. Optimize Ldata + λLclinical + (decay/2)Σθ² using Adam with coupled weight decay. FPG and BMI receive positive directions in A. Expanded directions are age+, SBP+, TG+ and HDL−. These priors permit exceptions. A flat network can satisfy them, so report meaningful changes and near-flat responses. [PyTorch autograd](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/stable/generated/torch.autograd.grad.html)

## Comparison, tuning and freezing

M1 is an unconstrained network with the same branch topology. M2 reuses M1 probabilities and explains that fit. M3 tunes the soft-constraint model. M1 and M3 receive identical base hyperparameter schedules, initialization seeds, batch order, stopping rules, candidate counts and training opportunities. M3 also searches λ in {0,1,10,100}. With few trials, these schedules sample a joint search rather than a complete factorial grid. A selected λ of zero is an admissible primary outcome.

Version 2 adds a secondary matched penalty comparison in the three-year risk task. For each outer development partition of the primary nested RICH analysis and for the final full-RICH development fit, use M3's selected non-penalty settings and epoch count. Within that development partition, compare λ in {1,10,100} on its existing inner splits and choose the lowest pooled validation IPCW Brier score. Ties select the smaller positive λ. Use identical splits, preprocessing rules, initialization seeds, batch order and fixed training epochs for `fixed_zero` and `fixed_positive`. Refit both on the same development data and freeze the final pair before evaluating it in NAGALA. The secondary contrast is Brier(fixed_positive) minus Brier(fixed_zero), with the same paired bootstrap procedure as the primary contrast. Both comparisons report the selected penalty strengths. This addition was planned after observing the zero-penalty SMOKE result and before the full scientific run.

M4 adds training-only site weighting. Pool sites with fewer than 50 training records. For K pooled sites of size nₛ, set aₛ=min(3,N/(K nₛ)) and divide by the participant-weighted mean of a. Multiply IPCW by the resulting aₛ. Retain the full batch denominator. Only the data term changes. M5 adds 0.25 times the squared difference of the two observed-sex mean weighted data losses in each batch containing both groups. This fixed exploratory coefficient avoids external selection. Its meaning depends on group event risk, censoring and missingness. Training loss parity provides no assurance of equal sensitivity, equal benefit or international adaptation.

Run `fpg_only`, `bmi_only` and `expanded_prior` at the selected positive strength and compare each with `fixed_positive`. Each changes only the set of prior directions. Other component runs use the selected primary reference configuration. Compare the four-input unconstrained model with M1, expanded inputs with M3 and M5 with M4. Report the selected λ with every comparison. Missingness, broader RICH eligibility and leave-one-observed-site-out analyses have separately identified outputs.

LR, RF, XGBoost, LightGBM and a linear SVM consume nonzero IPCW with explicit sample weights. The SVM probability map uses weighted logistic calibration of training-only cross-fitted scores from two development subfolds, each with its own preprocessing. The final SVM uses all its training data. The intercept-only reference is the weighted training event fraction. Ridge Cox uses min(T,3), includes events at 3, excludes later events from the event indicator, handles ties using Efron and estimates the Breslow baseline cumulative hazard in training. Convert to risk as 1−exp(−H₀(3)exp(η)). Constant training columns are removed from the Cox design. Secondary survival-curve endpoints are inactive. [lifelines CoxPHFitter](https://lifelines.readthedocs.io/en/latest/fitters/regression/CoxPHFitter.html)

Persist person-disjoint folds before fitting. Stratify by known case, known control or unknown and observed site code where counts permit. Pool rare sites within status. Each winning candidate minimizes summed held-out Brier contributions divided by total inner-validation N. Early stopping uses the inner validation Brier. Refit on the entire training partition for the rounded median selected epoch count across inner folds. The outer fold is scored only after selection. Final selection repeats the inner procedure on RICH. The primary comparison is between independently tuned M1 and M3, with fixed-architecture comparison as a secondary component test.

Thresholds come from source inner out-of-fold predictions of the winning candidate. Take the weighted 90th percentile of known-control scores, and classify positive strictly above that value (implemented with nextafter and ≥). Ties can give specificity above the target. FULL averages three neural seed probabilities equally. Apply the locked source threshold to that ensemble, report its actual specificity and keep per-seed results separate. All classical fits use one final seed. Include model artifacts, pipeline, parameter choices, stopping epoch, seed and hashes in the private freeze manifest before scoring the external cohort.

## Estimation and interpretation

**Primary external contrast.** Compute Δ = Brier₃(M3) − Brier₃(M1) in NAGALA, using Σw(y−p)²/N. Negative favors M3. Draw paired participant bootstrap samples of the same size with replacement. Use the same draw for both frozen predictions and re-estimate G on each draw. Percentile 95% intervals describe uncertainty conditional on training and this evaluation cohort. A null or unfavorable contrast is reportable. Retain failures and require at least 80% valid replicates (minimum two) per interval. SMOKE intervals are software checks. Site-held-out results describe observed groups. Folds and seeds do not form an independent sample for a t-test.

Secondary measures include cumulative/dynamic weighted AUROC, weighted average precision using stepwise recall increments, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, F1, FPR and FNR. Report the full-sample mean prediction and Σwy/N observed risk. Compute calibration-in-the-large by fitting a logistic intercept with the prediction logit as an offset; estimate the slope in a separate weighted logistic fit with a free intercept. Exact boundary logits are clipped to [10⁻⁷,1−10⁻⁷] only for these diagnostic regressions and intercept updating. Decile bins use all evaluation predictions with ties retained. Plot the bin mean probability against the weighted fraction Σwy/Σw, and report the participant-count-weighted mean absolute bin gap. Calibration slopes can be unavailable for constant predictions, one-class support or nonconvergence. Brier is a proper error measure that combines several predictive properties. [Average precision definition](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score.html)

Sex, ages 20–39/40–59/60+, RICH site, FPG <5.6, and missingness groups include denominators and known outcomes. Require 20 known cases and 20 known controls for detailed group estimates. This is an instability screen. Subgroup bootstraps resample participants within group and re-estimate group G. RICH OOF intervals condition on the fitted fold models and omit refitting uncertainty. Conditional-censoring assumptions may be unreliable in small strata. Other contrasts are descriptive/exploratory. There is one designated primary comparison and no confirmatory family of secondary hypothesis tests. Five- and seven-year primary claims remain inactive.

For explanations, choose up to 512 evaluation records with a fixed outcome-blind seed. Integrated Gradients uses 64 midpoint steps on the probability scale, a training-median reference and the record's original masks along the path. Check completeness against the reference probability and report the residual. Save five examples privately. Their attributions account for changes in measured values conditional on the masks. Mean absolute and signed contributions are separate from signed gradients. RF probability SHAP, when enabled, uses up to 64 source background rows and 32 evaluation examples. [Integrated Gradients](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/sundararajan17a.html)

Use raw-unit perturbations of +0.1 mmol/L FPG, +1 BMI unit, +1 year, +5 mmHg SBP, +0.1 mmol/L TG or HDL. Both ends stay inside training support. Report signed wrong-way frequency and the mean absolute wrong-way probability movement including zeros. A 0.001 absolute probability change is the operational meaningful-change screen; ≤10⁻⁶ is nearly flat. Compare central differences at ±0.005 training SD to analytical gradients after scaling back. Tolerances are 3×10⁻⁵ absolute standardized derivative and 1.5% relative. A finite clinical step and a local tangent can differ in a curved region. ICE sweeps training support for up to 24 observed examples. Individual curves and example tables are private. Changes in predictions under hypothetical measurements describe model sensitivity.

If enabled, target updating runs after frozen reporting. Fix a random split of 1,000 updating participants and the remainder for separate evaluation. Estimate only a logistic intercept offset with updating-set IPCW. Compare frozen and updated M3 probabilities on identical remaining people using the frozen threshold. Skip for inadequate updating support. This experiment does not change the primary frozen comparison.
